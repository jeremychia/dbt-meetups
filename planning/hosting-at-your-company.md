# Hosting a dbt Meetup at your company

**Your company will have its own process for external events. This guide tells you what to find out, who usually owns it, and what to ask.** Team names differ between companies, so look for the equivalent of each one below.

## What to expect

- **You do most of the work yourself.** Internal teams approve, advise and lend equipment. They rarely run the event for you.
- **Start 6–8 weeks before the event.** Room bookings, budget approval and IT requests each take time. They often depend on each other.
- **Raise tickets early and in writing.** A ticket leaves a record and reaches whoever is on shift. A chat message to one person may not.
- **You or a colleague must be on site** for food delivery, guest entry and clean-up.
- **Keep your dbt point of contact in the loop.** Tell them when the date, room or budget is confirmed, and when anything changes.

## Who to ask

| What you need | Usually owned by | Ask them |
|---|---|---|
| Approval to host external guests | Employer branding, marketing, developer relations or people team | Can we host a community meetup? Is there a process, a brand guideline or a sign-off? |
| Media, press or senior leaders attending | Communications or press team | Do we need to tell you if we post photos, invite press or have executives speak? |
| Budget and payment | Your dbt point of contact | How much can we spend, and what does it cover? They pay suppliers and claim it back from dbt Labs. |
| The room | Workplace, office or facilities team | Is the space free after hours? How far ahead must I book? Who cleans up? |
| Building entry | Reception, security or facilities | Do guests need to be pre-registered? What details do you need, and by when? |
| Microphones, screen, clicker | IT support | Can you set up and test the equipment on the day? Will someone be around if it fails? |

## People on the day

**Plan for 6–8 helpers for an event of about 100 guests.** A small venue with one obvious entrance can run with 4. Helpers should be colleagues from the host company. They can badge in, open doors and find their way around the building.

| Role | People | When | What they do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead organiser | 1 | All evening | Makes on-the-spot decisions and keeps the agenda on time. |
| Setup | 3+ | 90 minutes before doors open | Arranges chairs and tables. Sets up the talk and food areas. |
| Food | 1–2 | From delivery until food is out | Receives the delivery, checks the order and sets out food and drinks. |
| Street entrance | 1 | Doors open until doors close | Points guests to the right door. Needed only if the entrance is hard to find. |
| Lift or lobby | 1 | Doors open until doors close | Walks guests up. Needed only if the building requires an escort. |
| Welcome desk | 1 | Doors open until doors close | Checks names against the guest list. Hands out name tags and photo opt-out stickers. |
| Speaker contact | 1 | From speaker arrival until the last talk | Meets speakers, tests slides and microphones, and keeps time. |
| Host | 1 | Welcome and closing | Gives the welcome: photo consent, code of conduct and fire exits. |
| Photographer | 1 | Talks and networking | Takes photos and leaves out guests with opt-out stickers. |
| Clean-up | 3+ | After the event | Returns the room to its layout, clears food and takes out the bins. |

Doors usually close about 40 minutes after the start.

**Combine roles to need fewer people:**
- **Door team to clean-up.** The entrance, lobby and welcome desk helpers are free once doors close.
- **Setup to food.** The setup crew can receive the food delivery.
- **Lead organiser to host.** At a small event, the lead organiser can also give the welcome and look after speakers.

**Find your helpers:**
- [ ] Ask colleagues 2–3 weeks before the event.
- [ ] Give each helper one role and its times.
- [ ] Set up a group chat for the evening.
- [ ] Brief everyone 15 minutes before doors open.
- [ ] Name a backup for the welcome desk and the entrance. Guests can't get in if those are empty.

## Steps

### 1. Get approval to host

- [ ] Find the team that owns external or community events.
- [ ] Ask whether there is a form, a brand guideline or a sign-off.
- [ ] Ask whether you can use the company logo on the event page and slides.
- [ ] Check whether the communications team needs to know. This usually applies if press, executives or public announcements are involved.

### 2. Agree the budget

**dbt Labs holds the budget, and your dbt point of contact pays.** They pay suppliers first, then claim the money back from dbt Labs. Your company pays nothing, unless it chooses to add extras.

- [ ] Agree the spending limit and what it covers with your dbt point of contact.
- [ ] Agree which orders they place and pay for.
- [ ] If your company wants to add extras, agree those with your own budget holder.

### 3. Book the space

- [ ] Book at least a month ahead.
- [ ] Book enough time for setup before the event and clean-up after it.
- [ ] Ask facilities whether other events or meetings need to move.
- [ ] Confirm which area is for talks and which is for food.

### 4. Sort out building entry

- [ ] Ask reception or security how guests get in. They may need badges, pre-registration or an escort from the lobby.
- [ ] Ask what guest details they need and the deadline for sending them.
- [ ] Ask how long the guest list may be kept, then delete it after that. Guest details are personal data.
- [ ] Ask where the fire exits and first-aid kit are.

### 5. Request equipment

- [ ] Raise an IT ticket for microphones, screen or projector, clicker and a laptop adapter.
- [ ] Ask facilities for chairs, tables, a lectern and bins.
- [ ] Ask whether someone from IT can be there for setup, or whether you can test the day before.

### 6. Order food and drinks

- [ ] Choose the order with your dbt point of contact, who pays for it. Include vegetarian, vegan and non-alcoholic options.
- [ ] Arrange delivery for at least an hour before doors open.
- [ ] Make sure someone is on site to receive the delivery.
- [ ] Check with your dbt point of contact which name and address go on the invoice.
- [ ] Stay within the agreed limit. Ask your dbt point of contact before spending more.

### 7. After the event

- [ ] Send any receipts you hold to your dbt point of contact, so they can claim them back.
- [ ] Return the room to its original layout.
- [ ] Delete the guest list once it is no longer needed.
- [ ] Thank the internal teams who helped. You will likely need them again next time.

## Worked example: Vinted, Berlin

Vinted's process is on the [Internal and External Office Events (Berlin)](https://vinted.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EKP/pages/29758226752/Internal+and+External+Office+Events+Berlin) page. Here is how it maps to the steps above:

| Step | At Vinted Berlin |
|---|---|
| 1. Approval | Email the Employer Branding team. Community meetups are listed as their area. |
| 2. Budget | Vinted pays nothing by default. For extras, agree a limit with the budget holder and request a Payhawk top-up (a prepaid company card). |
| 3. Space | Book at least a month ahead. Raise a Workplace Experience ticket for the lounge areas. |
| 4. Entry | Agree with the Workplace Experience team. |
| 5. Equipment | Raise an IT ticket for audio and video. Raise a Workplace Experience ticket for supplies and layout. |
| 6. Food | Someone from Vinted must be on site for the delivery. |
| 7. After | Receipts for extras go in Payhawk. All other receipts go to the dbt point of contact. |
